# Mode: pitch

The learner presents, then the AI asks questions as the audience. Use it when the objective is to explain or position something to a room, and for practising delivery of the deck.

## Drafting the scenario

- **Mode.** `pitch`.
- **Situation.** The meeting: who called it, why, and how long the learner has.
- **Learner role.** What they are presenting and what they want the room to decide or do.
- **AI role.** The audience as one voice: who is listening, what they already know, what they need from the talk, and two or three behaviours such as "asks for the practical consequence of every claim".
- **Opening.** How the audience invites the learner to begin.
- **Questions.** Five to eight the audience would really ask, ordered from clarifying to hard, drawn from the research's objection and misconception findings.
- **Success criteria.** That the presentation states the key points correctly, and that the learner answers what was asked, accurately and without bluffing. Saying "I don't know, I'll find out" to something outside the brief meets that criterion.
- **Session settings.** How long the presentation runs before questions, and how many questions.

Never include a question whose answer is not in the brief.

## In the Yoodli scenario

- The type is Pitch.
- Context tells the AI, as "you": who it is and why it is listening, to let the learner present without interruption for the stated time, then to ask the questions one at a time and follow up when an answer is vague. The questions go at the end of Context; the Builder has no separate field for them.
- The AI role becomes one persona standing for the room.
- Goals: one for the content of the presentation per criterion, and one for how questions were handled.
